ANSWER:
Based on the provided documents, LLMs handle refusal by utilizing categorical refusal tokens, which are generated by a fine-tuned model called REFUSE-LLAMA. This model can produce either a response token or a category-specific refusal token before delivering a message, allowing for differentiation between types of refusals [Passage 3][Passage 5]. 

Additionally, the evaluation of refusal behavior is conducted using an LLM-as-a-judge, specifically LLAMA 3.3 70B INSTRUCT, which counts the number of refusals in assistant responses [Passage 1]. The labeling of refusals is also corroborated by human annotators who follow the same criteria as the LLM judge [Passage 2].

However, the documents do not provide detailed explanations of how refusal tokens specifically influence the responses or the underlying mechanisms of refusal behavior in LLMs.
