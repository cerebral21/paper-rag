ANSWER:
Categorical steering vectors are specific directional vectors that are extracted from the residual-stream activations in the context of refusal token fine-tuning. They are designed to steer the model's responses toward or away from specific types of refusal during inference.

These vectors are constructed by utilizing categorical refusal tokens to extract multiple category-specific steering vectors from the residual-stream activations. This process involves fine-tuning the refusal tokens, which improves the category structure of the residual stream, leading to better-separated activation groupings that align with the refusal taxonomy. This makes it easier to extract the corresponding categorical steering directions [Passage 3][Passage 4].

The construction of these vectors is facilitated by a lightweight probe that determines, at inference time, whether a response should be steered toward or away from refusal based on the residual-stream activations from an optimal layer [Passage 6]. Additionally, the categorical steering vectors are normalized to ensure they have a unit length, which is part of the preparation for their application in steering [Passage 8].

In summary, categorical steering vectors are derived from the residual-stream activations through a process that involves fine-tuning refusal tokens and utilizing a linear probe to guide the steering direction during inference.
